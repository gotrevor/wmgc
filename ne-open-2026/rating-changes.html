Micah Steinberg got the most out of the least: +0.06136 was the smallest gain of any four-win player here, and it was enough because he started five hundredths of a point from the line. He is now a 3k by eight thousandths, the third-weakest of 562 3k players in the country.

Forest Song moved least of anyone who played - five games, 3-2, and a change of +0.00201. That is what a correctly rated player looks like.

Eight demotions

Eight players lost a rank. Five of them went 1-4 and one went 0-5; two dropped two ranks at once. Across everyone who lost points, the average was -0.27.

One demotion is worth naming, because it is the strangest line in the file: Jeremy Chiu went 4-1, won the tournament, and lost a stone. 8.03208 → 7.98574, a change of -0.04634 - and 0.032 was all the 8d interval he had left. He was the fourth-weakest of 96 8d players nationally going in; he is the third-strongest of 260 7d players coming out.

Did the weekend's results predict the rating change?

Monotonically, yes: each additional win moves the column average up, from -1.84 at no wins to +0.65 at four. But the spread inside a column is wider than the gap between columns. Who you played matters as much as how often you won.

Method: AGA TDList snapshots from before and after the September 7 rating update, joined to the submitted results file on AGA ID. Rating differences are normalized - the AGA scale skips the open interval (-1, 1), so dan ratings are shifted down 2 and ranks indexed continuously (2k=-2, 1k=-1, 1d=0, 2d=1). Without that, a 1k→1d promotion reads as +2 for what is really +0.4 of movement. Ranks truncate toward zero: 4.99 is a 4d. National band positions were computed across all rated players in the September 7 list.
